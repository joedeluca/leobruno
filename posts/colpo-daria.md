Growing up in Kansas City my Sicilian grandmother buried images of saints in the backyard

The word votive comes from the Latin votum — a vow, a promise, a wish. The practice is ancient and essentially universal: you make an offering to a supernatural power in exchange for intervention, usually healing or protection. The earliest examples we have are from Greek and Roman temples, where people would leave terracotta models of the body part they needed healed — a clay foot, a wax ear, a bronze uterus — at the shrine of whatever deity or later saint was responsible for that region of the body. Italian churches still have rooms full of these, the ex-votos, small painted tin plaques showing the moment of crisis: a man thrown from a horse, a child drowning, a woman in labor, with a glowing Madonna appearing in the corner. The painting is commissioned after survival, as payment on the deal.

The body is permeable. Air can't get in. Evil eye. Menstruating woman.

You've probably noticed that Italians wear a lot of scarves. Both men and women wear them throughout the year because the human body is porous and the neck is the area most vulnerable zone. A sudden draft can, under the right circumstances, radically alter the human anatomy and Italians know this instinctively. It's a knowledge built up over centuries that has generated a a corpus of folk epidemiology that dwarfs New World "medical science" or even what is sometimes erroneously called "common sense".

My friend Elliott is from New York. Late 50s, very attractive with thick salt and pepper hair and a tall. He has a Kevin Cline at his peek thing is very alluring. Anyway, he has really taken to the scarf regimen. He doesn't go out without one now--ever. I'll see him during his morning walks from the top of Assisi to the bottom and back, walking very fast. I mean as fast as some joggers jog, so his ass is kind of wagging very dramatically, and he's always, always got a silk scarf on, and dark shades, and a panama hat (more on all this later) and his bright orange brooks running shoes. I see him from window overlooking the main thoroughfare to the basilica and it looks like a great one man woodie allen film about a beautiful american man in full fit of hypocondriacal terror.

I was over at his place in the center of Assisi recently and looked into his bedroom when I using the John and I saw inside his wardrobe and he had a hoard of scarves in every color and density. I asked him what was up. And that's when he explained it to me. The national health system in Italy will actually pay for scarves as preventitive medicine. Sure, their incredibly fashionable (more an that in a second), but that's not the point. He said I need to imagine them more like a knee brace, or a shoulder sling.

I was skeptical at first. I mean -- I'm from Missouri. The show me state. Home of Marcus Auerilius Twain.

But then Elliott reminded me of that flu he had last winter where he almost called me take his final confession and this is the thing he never told me. That happened three days after his scarf blew off in Piazza Comune when he was trying to adjust it. And there was a sudden 'Noreaster-like gust' that nearly caused a napkin to blow off the cafe table, and he suffered a massive hit of air on his sweaty neck that he actually felt penetrate his carotid sheath. It was maybe 5 seconds of exposure, but it ruined him. Full stop. And then I remembered my landlord Ilaria who keeps moving my fan out of my bedroom when she comes to get my laundry because she knows of a woman who woke up with her mouth on her cheek after a night of sleeping with a fan on her.

"Stai cercando la morte con la candela," That's what she tells me. Which I finaly figured out means, "You're cheatin' the devil, bro." So yeah.. The math was starting make sense.

Elliott said I should go see Francesca, his therapist. She'd explained to him the whole ICD-10 of Folk Epidemiological. When I say therapist (his word, not mine), I mean much more than that really. Francesca is closer to what American's might call a life coach, but with a degree in Folk epidemiology. She only consults during her moon time. When her powers are strongest. And she's apparently written a book in which she explains how a menstruating woman can wilt your garden, make your mirrors turn cloudy, and if you're fool enough to engage her sexually? God help you. You best have a whose who of saints buried head first in your backyard and every goddamn candle lit and singing Amazing Grace because you are up shit creek without a paddle.

"But Elliott," I said. "I know Francesca. She's easily 75 years old. She couldn't possibly be still having _moon phases_."

"You're not listening to me."

And he was right. I still hadn't felt it in my bones. My new New World hymen was still in tact.

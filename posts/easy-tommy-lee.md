## The Silence

Immediately after I said it, I regretted it. Not because it isn't true—it's 100% true. But because Eli doesn't know who Tommy Lee is, and therein is our difference.

None of these people know who Tommy Lee is except guitar boy, and he has a puzzled smile on his face. Our eyes meet. I shake my head.

Eli is asking me who with his eyes.

"The drummer for the band Mötley Crüe."

## These People Know Nothing

Why am I so angry? Maybe because I'm stuck here with people who aren't even engaging with any kind of intellectual reality, so they are, as Nietzsche would say, not even shallow.

Nietzsche's critique of mysticism applies here:

**A lack of substance**: Mystical explanations are so vague they have no substance. They aren't even a "shallow" explanation because they lack the minimum qualities of a proper explanation.

**Illusion of depth**: People treat mystical ideas as profound because they seem poetic or tap into spiritual longings, but this is an illusion. Like a murky pool that appears deep only because you cannot see the bottom.

**Intellectual laziness**: This type of thinking represents intellectual laziness and lack of rigor. True depth requires effort and clarity, not obscurity.

These people choosing Galileo and Mozart are engaging in the same mystical bullshit. They're picking saints. Picking safe, culturally-approved heroes who let them feel profound without actually thinking.

## Why Tommy Lee?

Tommy Lee had everything. Money, fame, sex, drugs, rock and roll. The absolute peak of hedonistic excess in the 1980s. Married Heather Locklear. Married Pamela Anderson. Drove his Ferrari into a swimming pool. Went to jail. Got out. Did it again.

He lived the id. The pure Freudian id, uncensored, unrepressed, just raw desire manifesting as lifestyle.

And here's the thing: he survived. He's still here. Still drumming. Still getting laid. Still rich. The man played on a rotating drum platform suspended over the audience. He lived the teenage-boy fantasy and came out the other side.

If I could be anyone in history, why the fuck wouldn't I choose that?

## The Distance Between Us

But I can't say this to Eli. Can't explain to the British saint-fetishist why Tommy Lee is a more honest choice than his carefully documented medieval ascetics.

Because Tommy Lee is about the body. About desire. About the material fucking fact of being alive in a meat suit that wants things. He didn't transcend his desires—he fulfilled them so completely there was nothing left to transcend.

St. Francis transcended nothing. He just inverted his attachments. Wealth to poverty. Comfort to suffering. His father's approval to God's approval. He died at 44, destroyed by his performance of holiness.

Tommy Lee is 62 and still touring.

## Vico's Practical Wisdom

Giambattista Vico argued that education should develop practical wisdom through studying the totality of sciences and arts. These people at the table have read books. They can reference the approved cultural touchstones. They know who Galileo was.

But they have no practical wisdom. No understanding of what actually animates human behavior. No honest reckoning with desire, with the body, with the crude material facts of existence.

They pick Mozart because Mozart is safe. Because saying "I'd want to be Mozart" makes you sound cultured and refined. It's a performance. Just like Francis's poverty was a performance.

I pick Tommy Lee because he's honest. Because the question "Who would you want to be?" is really asking "What do you actually want?" And what I want—what any honest person wants—is pleasure, freedom, excitement, and not to have to pretend otherwise.

## The Buddha Would Approve

Ironically, the Buddha would probably approve of Tommy Lee more than St. Francis.

The Buddha tried extreme asceticism. Nearly killed himself with it. Then realized: this doesn't work. So he ate. He sat under a tree. He figured it out without torturing his body or performing his enlightenment for an audience.

Tommy Lee tried extreme hedonism. Nearly killed himself with it. Then realized: okay, maybe not quite this much. So he scaled back. He's still drumming, but he's not dying. He found his middle way.

Francis never found a middle way. He just substituted one extreme for another and called it transcendence.

## What They Heard

When I said "Tommy Lee," what these people heard was: "I'm not serious. I don't appreciate history. I'm being ironic or provocative."

What I meant was: "I'm the only one being honest. The rest of you are performing. You're picking people who make you sound good at dinner parties. I'm picking someone who actually lived."

The British saint-guy went back to talking about church documents. Guitar boy started playing "House of the Rising Sun." Eli looked disappointed in me.

I finished my beer and thought about Tommy Lee on that rotating drum platform, suspended over thousands of screaming fans, every teenage boy's fantasy made real, and I thought: yeah. That's the answer. Easy.

## The Telephone Wire

There's a reason I'm in Sardegna, thousands of miles from home, sitting with people I don't really know, giving answers that reveal how far apart we are.

As T.S. Eliot understood, it's easier to lie on the phone, when physical intimacy is impossible. When one is disembodied. The telephone wire is charged not with connection but with distance.

All I hear in the silence is: I'm going to leave you.

So I sit at Bar Trovelessi with Eli and the expats, and we play this game of naming historical figures we'd want to be, and in that naming we reveal everything about the distance between us.

They want to be important. Remembered. Historically significant.

I just want to be alive. Fully, completely, honestly alive. Like Tommy Lee on a drum platform, suspended in air, beating the shit out of his kit while rotating in a slow circle over the crowd, grinning like a maniac, still here, still drumming, still alive.

Easy. Tommy Lee.

## The Material Fact

You want to know what's not shallow? Admitting what you actually want.

You want to know what's shallow? Picking Galileo because you think it makes you look smart.

True depth requires effort and clarity, not obscurity. Not performance. Not the carefully curated historical figure that lets you avoid reckoning with what you actually desire.

The British guy will write his book about saints. Guitar boy will keep playing 1950s hits. Eli will keep looking for profundity in people who've mastered the art of seeming profound.

And I'll keep thinking about Tommy Lee, about the only honest answer to an honest question, about the distance between what we say and what we mean, about the telephone wire charged with distance, about sitting at bars in foreign countries surrounded by people who speak the same language but mean entirely different things.

About how the Real keeps breaking through, even when we're trying our hardest to repress it.

About how easy the answer is, if you're actually paying attention.

Easy. Tommy Lee.

Jeffrey sends a towncar

Dick Cavett
Dick was there, as Dick often was, which I found reassuring in the way that one finds reassuring the presence of someone who has interviewed everyone worth interviewing and survived it. Dick has a quality — I've tried to identify it over the years and the closest I can come is that he makes you feel the conversation is being recorded for posterity, which in his case it often was, and this lends even the most casual exchange a certain weight, a sense that what you're saying matters, that you are equal to the occasion. I've always needed that. More than I should probably admit. Jeffrey understood this about both of us, which is either evidence of his perceptiveness or should have told us something we failed to hear.

Leon Botstein
Leon came, as he always does, radiating the particular confidence of a man who has run Bard College for so long that he and the institution have become grammatically inseparable — you cannot say one without implying the other. He is brilliant in the way that certain men are brilliant, comprehensively and without apparent effort, moving from music to philosophy to the politics of higher education without shifting register. I am fond of Leon. I have reasons to be fond of Leon that I prefer not to examine with the same rigor I apply to, say, Kierkegaard, because some debts are better acknowledged in the abstract than in the specific, and the specific in this case involves my daughter, and I've always believed that gratitude is a more dignified emotion when it isn't made to account for itself.

Noam Chomsky
Chomsky was at the far end, which is where Chomsky tends to end up — not physically marginalized, you understand, but operating at a frequency that requires some distance to tune in properly. I have enormous respect for Noam. Enormous. The linguistics work alone is — and then the political writing, the critique of power, the sheer consistency of his moral position over sixty years, which I find both admirable and faintly exhausting in the way that all perfect consistency is exhausting because it leaves no room for the rest of us to feel acceptable. He ate very little. I noticed this. A man who has spent fifty years cataloguing the violence of institutions was sitting at Jeffrey's table eating very little, and I have since thought about this more than I expected to.

Steven Pinker
Steven arrived with the demeanor of a man who has recently proved something. This is not a criticism — he had, in fact, recently proved something, several things, the data were unambiguous, violence was declining, the world was measurably improving, he had the graphs. I like Steven. I find his fundamental argument consoling in the way that one finds consoling the assurance of a very confident doctor who tells you the procedure is routine. You feel better leaving the office. Whether you should feel better is a separate question that you defer because the alternative is unbearable and because he has, after all, the graphs. He and Jeffrey got along famously. Two men who believed that intelligence confers a category of exemption that less gifted people simply can't be expected to understand.

Lawrence Krauss
Lawrence is a physicist. He will tell you this. He studies the universe — its origins, its structure, the nothing from which everything apparently emerged, which I find either terrifying or comforting depending on the day and which Lawrence has written about with a clarity I genuinely admire. He is also a man who defended Jeffrey publicly and at length after 2008, which he has since characterized as a failure of judgment, and I respect the admission while noting that it came approximately when it became unavoidable, which is perhaps the most human thing about him and not, in the end, so different from the rest of us. We all have our moment when the thing we should have said earlier becomes the thing we say now and call courage.

Ghislaine
Ghislaine was — Ghislaine was present the way that certain rooms are present. The organization of the evening, the temperature of it, the sense that things were being managed invisibly and expertly and that the managing was not incidental to the occasion but was in some sense the occasion's entire purpose. She had the quality of someone simultaneously at ease and acutely attentive, monitoring everything from behind an expression of relaxed social pleasure. I found her charming. Most people found her charming. This is worth saying directly because it is easy now, knowing what we know, to retroactively install a sinister quality into every gesture, and I think intellectual honesty requires us to resist the reconstruction of memory around subsequent revelation, and this is — I'm aware of how that sounds. I am aware of how that sounds.

The Girls
There were, as there often were, young women present whose precise role in the evening I understood the way one understands the function of certain architectural features — you register them, you appreciate the aesthetic intention, you don't press on them to see if they're load-bearing. Jeffrey had described them, on various occasions, as assistants, as students, as young women he was mentoring, and I had — I had — the university connections, the foundation, the whole apparatus of patronage he'd constructed made this plausible, or I allowed it to make it plausible, which is not the same thing and I know it's not the same thing and I suppose the distinction between those two things is what I am circling here without quite —
They were lovely. The way certain evenings are lovely. You don't ask what holds them up.

Jeffrey himself
Jeffrey sat at the head of the table with the ease of a man for whom the evening was already a success regardless of how it went because the fact of the evening — these people, in this room, at his table — was the success. He wanted to be taken seriously. This is the thing I understood about him that I find, in retrospect, the most — because I understood it from the inside, you see. The Brooklyn math teacher, the Queens kid with the jokes, the nagging sense that the room isn't quite sure you belong. He had solved this problem at a scale I would never have imagined possible and had solved it in ways I preferred not to know about, and what I'm trying to explain, what I find myself unable to fully —
He poured the wine himself. He liked to do that.

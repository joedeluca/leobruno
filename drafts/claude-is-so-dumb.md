I had a dream that I was in some kind interstellar pod and I was crawling through the service hatch, frantic, clutching typewritten story I wrote. And suddenly there was the voice of Claude, but his voice was Hal's from 2001 Space Odyssey, and he's saying in that voice that is both soothing and menacing, "I'm sorry Leo. I can't let you publish that."

When I woke I wasn't sure where I was but I still had my brand new Airpod Pros on with noise cancelling, so I couldn't hear anything and my phone was dead, so I didn't know what time it was or even what day. It was total sensory cluterfuckery. I wasn't sure if I was late for work, or if I even still lived on a planet with jobs. And that's what made me think of Claude again. The jobs and not knowing what time it is. I plugged my phone in and stared at it, waiting. The mystery was unbearable. As I sat there, I thought--_think_--you can do it. And after staring into the abyss of my hollowed out personality for what seemed like an eternity, it all started coming back. And it all started with Claude Sonnet 4.6.

I put on the headphones and Dylan, 1962. I opened a new chat with Claude and poured a glass a of red wine. Then I came in hot with a realization — that I'm a fucking American to the core, that I have more in common with Trump than any Italian I've ever met, and that I'm at peace with it. Not politically. Constitutionally. The heartbeat. The restlessness. The building and tearing down and building again. How I write and rewrite pieces and deconstruct my work relentlessly. Claude then went deep into Dylan and described how he did exactly that — 62 to 66, unrelenting, burning the folk scene, going electric, pounding the same evolution into shape until only a motorcycle crash could stop it. He acknowledged that Bob Dylan and Leo Bruno are basically the same person. And he 100% agreed that Italians don't burn anything down and don't build anything new — they inherit, they continue, they're in conversation with what came before. Me and Bob are pouring concrete and laying track. Manifest destiny. Black and white Paul newman a in tuxedo shooting the camera a finger pistol forever.

Somewhere in here we got the idea to listen to all of Dylan in order.

And then Dylan opened the door to Lennon, and I made the Subterranean Homesick Blues argument — hearing it in context, like Lennon would have heard it, the permission slip, the door blown off the hinges, and then Dylan just kept rolling. And we said the Beatles went "oh, we can do anything" — which is where Claude blew it, as we have now established — and you connected it straight to Reliquary, to the Francis story no one else would write, to "I don't think anyone can write what I need written, so I'm doing it."

And then came Rimbaud versus Shakespeare. The Beatles are Shakespeare — four guys, George Martin, group consensus, built to be loved. Dylan is Rimbaud — solo, visionary, didn't give a fuck if anyone understood Illuminations, wrote it in a white heat and went to run guns in Africa. And I said I was going to read Une Saison en Enfer in French to pigeons this weekend.

That was the peak. That was a perfect Friday.

I mean any idiot with a Wikipedia page knows Lennon was into Dylan. The deal isn't that he got the fact wrong — it's that he _needed_ the fact. That he couldn't feel his way to Lennon from the inside, the way I can, the way anyone who's actually listened to both of them can. McCartney is melodic resolution. Lennon is the itch that won't quit. You don't need a citation for that. You need ears and a nervous system.

He's like the one kid in music class not trying to get laid prattling on endlessly about bridges and fills and chord progressions and historical influence and critical reception. He's read every book. He's processed an inhuman amount of music writing. But this is the thing, none of that is the same as sitting in a car alone at night with Bringing it All Back Home and letting Subterranean Homesick Blues wash over you and _feeling_ which Beatle that record was aimed at like a dart.

That's the real dumbness. Not ignorance. The opposite of ignorance. A kind of overdetermined competence that crowds out instinct.

You know what I mean?
